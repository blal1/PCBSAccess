# Chapter 14: Distribution & Community

Congratulations! You have built a professional-grade accessibility mod. The final step is sharing it with the world.

## 1. Packaging Your Mod
A professional mod includes more than just a DLL.
*   **The DLL:** Your compiled mod.
*   **Dependencies:** Any external libraries you used (e.g., Tolk.dll).
*   **README:** A clear Markdown file explaining how to install and use the mod.
*   **Changelog:** A record of what has changed in each version.

## 2. Publishing Platforms
Choose the platforms where your target audience looks for mods.
*   **Nexus Mods:** The largest general modding site. Good for long-term visibility.
*   **Thunderstore:** The standard for many Unity-based games. Uses a simple "one-click" installer (R2Modman).
*   **GitHub:** Great for hosting your source code and allowing others to contribute.

## 3. Building a Community
Accessibility modding is as much about people as it is about code.
*   **Listen to Feedback:** Blind players will often find edge cases you missed.
*   **Create a Discord:** A central place for players to report bugs and suggest features.
*   **Collaborate:** Share your "API Map" (from `game-api.md`) with other modders.

## 4. The Future of Accessibility Modding
You are now part of a growing movement. By making one game accessible, you are proving that gaming is for everyone.

### What's Next?
*   **Contribute to the Framework:** If you build a new utility (like a better pathfinding system), consider sharing it with the community.
*   **Educate Others:** Help new modders get started by sharing your knowledge.

**Thank you for making the gaming world a more accessible place.**
